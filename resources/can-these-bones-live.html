Sermon Reflection

When Hope Feels Lost

Discovering New Life in the Valley of Dry Bones

Scripture: Ezekiel 37:1–14

“Mortal, can these bones live?”

There are moments when life feels like a valley of dry bones.

Hope feels distant. The future feels uncertain. What once seemed full of life now feels scattered, broken, and beyond repair.

In Ezekiel 37, God leads the prophet into a valley filled with dry bones. These bones represent a people who had lost almost everything. Their land, their sense of security, their hope, and their future all seemed gone.

Then God asks Ezekiel a question:

“Can these bones live?”

It is a question that still speaks to us today.

Can hope return after deep disappointment? Can healing come after trauma? Can God restore what appears lifeless? Can new life rise from places that seem forgotten?

God Meets Us in the Valley

One of the powerful truths of this passage is that God does not avoid the valley.

God does not pretend the bones are not dry. He does not minimize the loss. He does not rush past the pain.

Instead, God speaks life into the very place where hope seems impossible.

Bone comes to bone. Tendons form. Flesh appears. Breath enters. What was once lifeless begins to stand again.

The valley does not get the final word.

God does.

Hope for the Forgotten

In the message, the Valley of Dry Bones is connected to the ministry of the Kentucky United Methodist Children’s Homes.

Many children and young people arrive carrying stories of trauma, disappointment, crisis, and loneliness. Some have been through repeated foster care placements. Some have learned to expect rejection. Some have begun to believe that hope is no longer possible.

They may not use the words of Ezekiel, but their hearts may carry the same cry:

“Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost.”

Yet the message of Ezekiel is that hopelessness is not permanent.

God still restores. God still heals. God still breathes life into weary places. God still raises up people who thought their story was over.

The Church Has a Role

Ezekiel was not simply asked to observe the valley. He was called to speak into it.

That is part of the calling of the Church.

We are called to speak hope where hope feels lost. We are called to pray, serve, give, encourage, welcome, and participate in God’s restoring work.

Every act of compassion becomes a way of saying:

“These bones can live.”

When the Church supports ministries of healing and hope, we become part of God’s answer to despair.

We become witnesses that brokenness does not have to be the end of the story.

God Is Still Breathing Life

The hope of Ezekiel’s vision is ultimately fulfilled in the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

The cross looked like the end. It looked like defeat. It looked like the final word.

But God was not finished.

The empty tomb reminds us that God brings life out of death, hope out of despair, and new beginnings out of places that seem beyond repair.

So when we ask, “Can these bones live?” the Gospel answers:

Yes. These bones can live.

Reflection Questions

  • Where do you see “dry bones” in your life or community?
  • Where have you been tempted to believe hope is lost?
  • How might God be inviting you to speak hope into someone else’s valley?
  • What is one way you can support healing and restoration this week?

Prayer

God of hope, breathe life into the places where hope feels lost. Open our eyes to the valleys around us and give us courage to speak words of life, healing, and restoration. Help us to be part of Your work in the lives of children, families, neighbors, and all who feel forgotten. Remind us that no valley is beyond Your reach and no story is beyond Your redemption. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
