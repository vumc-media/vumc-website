Sermon Reflection

Can These Bones Live?

Finding Hope in Places That Seem Beyond Repair

Scripture: Ezekiel 37:1–14

There are moments in life when all we can see is what has been lost.

A relationship has fractured. A dream has faded. A church seems stuck. A community feels divided. A person carries wounds so deep that healing appears impossible.

When we look around, all we see are dry bones.

That is exactly where the prophet Ezekiel found himself.

In one of the most vivid visions in all of Scripture, God led Ezekiel into a valley filled with bones. Not a few scattered remains, but an entire valley stretching as far as the eye could see. These were not recently fallen bones. They were dry. Lifeless. Forgotten.

Then God asked a question:

“Mortal, can these bones live?”
— Ezekiel 37:3

What a strange question.

From a human perspective, the answer seems obvious. Bones do not come back to life. Dead things stay dead. Hope once lost rarely returns.

Yet God was inviting Ezekiel to see beyond what his eyes could see.

Standing in the Valley

Sometimes we find ourselves standing in valleys of our own.

Perhaps the dry bones represent a relationship that seems beyond repair. Maybe they symbolize a broken dream, a painful diagnosis, a struggling ministry, or a season of grief that refuses to loosen its grip.

Sometimes the bones are found within us.

Disappointment has a way of drying out our hope. Weariness can leave us feeling spiritually exhausted. Fear can convince us that tomorrow will simply look like yesterday.

We may not say it out loud, but our hearts begin to whisper the same words spoken by Israel:

“Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost.”
— Ezekiel 37:11

Yet God’s response is not condemnation.

It is restoration.

God Speaks Life

God tells Ezekiel to speak life into the valley. As the prophet obeys, something remarkable begins to happen.

Bones begin to move. They come together. Tendons form. Flesh appears. Breath enters. What was once lifeless becomes a vast multitude standing on its feet.

The vision is not merely about resurrection.

It is about hope.

It is about a God who specializes in bringing life where others see only death.

Throughout Scripture, God repeatedly demonstrates this truth.

Abraham and Sarah were too old to have children. Joseph sat forgotten in prison. Israel wandered in the wilderness. The disciples watched Jesus die on a cross.

Every situation seemed hopeless.

Yet God was not finished.

The Hope of Resurrection

The empty tomb reminds us that resurrection is not just a possibility; it is part of God’s character. The same God who breathed life into the valley of dry bones raised Jesus from the dead.

And that same God is still at work today.

Perhaps the most encouraging part of Ezekiel’s vision is that restoration did not happen all at once.

The bones came together first.

Then tendons.

Then flesh.

Then breath.

The process took time.

Many of us want instant transformation. We want God to fix everything immediately. Yet often God works through a process of healing, growth, and renewal.

Sometimes the first sign of life is simply taking the next faithful step.

Sometimes restoration begins with a conversation. A prayer. A phone call. An act of forgiveness. A decision to keep going when giving up feels easier.

The valley may still look barren, but God is already at work.

Can These Bones Live?

The question remains:

Can these bones live?

The Gospel answers with a resounding yes.

Not because of our strength. Not because of our wisdom. Not because circumstances suddenly become easy.

But because God’s Spirit still breathes life into weary hearts.

He still restores what appears broken. He still creates new beginnings. He still brings hope where hope seems lost.

Whatever valley you find yourself walking through today, remember this:

The God of Ezekiel’s vision has not changed.

The God of the empty tomb is still at work.

And the story is not over yet.

Reflection Questions

  1. What “dry bones” are present in your life right now?
  2. Where have you been tempted to believe that hope is lost?
  3. How might God be inviting you to trust Him in the midst of uncertainty?
  4. What signs of new life can you already see, even if they seem small?

Prayer

God of hope, there are times when we look around and see only dry bones. We see brokenness, disappointment, and situations that feel beyond repair. Remind us that You are a God of resurrection and renewal. Breathe Your Spirit into our weary hearts. Help us trust that You are still working, even when we cannot yet see the outcome. Give us faith to believe that what seems impossible with us is possible with You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
