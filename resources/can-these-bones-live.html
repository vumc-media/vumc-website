Devotional Reflection

Can These Bones Live?

Finding Hope When Everything Seems Beyond Repair

Scripture: Ezekiel 37:3

"He said to me, 'Mortal, can these bones live?' I answered, 'O Lord God, you know.'"

Some seasons of life feel like standing in a valley full of dry bones.

We look around and see relationships that seem broken beyond repair. Dreams that never materialized. Churches struggling to find their footing. Communities wounded by division. Hearts exhausted by disappointment.

Everywhere we look, we see evidence of what has been lost.

That is where Ezekiel found himself.

God led the prophet into a valley covered with bones—dry, lifeless, and scattered across the landscape. Then God asked a question that seemed almost absurd:

“Can these bones live?”

From a human perspective, the answer appears obvious.

Dead things stay dead. Broken things remain broken. Hope once lost rarely returns.

Yet God was inviting Ezekiel—and us—to see beyond what is visible.

The Valley Is Not the End of the Story

The remarkable thing about Ezekiel's vision is that God does not ignore the reality of the bones.

He doesn't pretend the valley isn't there. He doesn't minimize the devastation.

Instead, He speaks life into it.

Bone comes to bone. Tendons form. Flesh appears. Breath enters. What was once lifeless becomes a vast multitude standing on its feet.

The message is clear:

God specializes in bringing life where others see only death.

Hope for Today

Perhaps your dry bones are not literal.

Maybe they look like grief that still lingers. Anxiety about the future. A family conflict that refuses to heal. A ministry challenge that feels overwhelming.

Maybe you have quietly whispered the same words spoken by Israel:

“Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost.”

Yet the God who spoke life into Ezekiel's valley is still speaking today.

The God who raised Jesus from the dead continues to bring hope into hopeless situations, healing into wounded places, and purpose into broken stories.

The valley may be real, but it is not the final chapter.

A Word for the Church

One of the beautiful truths of this passage is that God often uses people to participate in His work of restoration.

Ezekiel was called to speak hope into the valley.

The Church is called to do the same.

Every act of compassion, every prayer offered, every child encouraged, every person welcomed, every meal served, every hand extended in grace becomes a declaration that these bones can live.

We become witnesses to God's power to restore what appears beyond repair.

Reflection Questions

  • What “dry bones” are present in your life right now?
  • Where have you been tempted to lose hope?
  • How might God be inviting you to trust Him in this season?
  • How can you bring hope to someone else's valley this week?

Prayer

God of hope, there are times when we look around and see only dry bones. We see brokenness, disappointment, and situations that seem beyond repair. Remind us that You are the God of resurrection and renewal. Breathe Your Spirit into our weary hearts. Give us faith to trust that You are still working, even when we cannot yet see the outcome. Help us become people who speak hope into the valleys around us. Through Christ our Lord, Amen.
