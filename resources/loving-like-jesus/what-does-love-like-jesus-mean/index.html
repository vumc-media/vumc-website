Loving Like Jesus • Week 1

What Does Love Like Jesus Mean?

Discovering the heart of Christ's command to love one another.

About This Series
Loving Like Jesus is a devotional series from Versailles United Methodist Church exploring what it means to follow Jesus in everyday life through scripture, reflection, prayer, and practical application.

Scripture Focus

John 13:34-35 (CEB)

"I give you a new commandment: Love each other. Just as I have loved you, so you also must love each other. This is how everyone will know that you are my disciples, when you love each other."

More Than a Church Slogan

At Versailles United Methodist Church, one phrase appears often:

Love Like Jesus.

You'll find it on our website, hear it in worship, and see it woven throughout our ministries. But what does it actually mean?

For many people, love has become a vague word. We say we love pizza, sports teams, favorite television shows, and family members. The word gets used so frequently that it can lose some of its depth.

When Jesus spoke about love, He wasn't talking about a feeling that comes and goes. He wasn't talking about agreement, popularity, or convenience. He was describing a way of living.

Jesus calls His followers to love as He loved.

That is both beautiful and challenging.

Jesus Loved People Where They Were

One of the remarkable things about Jesus is the people He chose to spend time with.

  • He shared meals with tax collectors.
  • He welcomed children.
  • He spoke with people others avoided.
  • He showed compassion to those who had failed.
  • He offered hope to people carrying shame.

Jesus never waited for people to become perfect before loving them.

He met them where they were.

Many people today carry wounds from life, relationships, disappointment, or even experiences with the church. Sometimes people assume they have to get everything together before God will welcome them.

The ministry of Jesus tells a different story.

God's love reaches toward us long before we have everything figured out.

Love Is More Than Being Nice

Sometimes we confuse love with simply being nice.

Jesus was kind, but He was also honest.

He comforted people who were hurting.

He challenged people who were wandering.

He offered grace and truth at the same time.

Real love does not ignore people. Real love does not use people. Real love does not seek its own advantage.

The love of Jesus seeks what is best for others.

Love in a Divided World

It doesn't take long to notice that our world is deeply divided.

Political differences divide people.

Social issues divide people.

Generational differences divide people.

Even churches can become divided.

Yet Jesus did not tell His disciples that people would recognize them by their political opinions, denominational labels, or personal preferences.

He said people would know they belonged to Him because of their love.

The church has an opportunity to offer something the world desperately needs: a community where people can encounter the love of Christ.

Loving Like Jesus Starts Small

Many people imagine following Jesus requires dramatic acts of faith.

Sometimes it does.

More often, it begins in ordinary moments:

  • A kind word.
  • A listening ear.
  • A note of encouragement.
  • A prayer for someone who is struggling.
  • A meal shared with a neighbor.
  • A willingness to forgive.

Small acts of love often become powerful testimonies of God's presence.

Prayer

Lord Jesus,

Thank You for loving us with patience, mercy, and grace. Teach us to love others the way You have loved us. Help us to see people through Your eyes and respond with compassion, kindness, and humility.

When it is difficult to love, give us strength. When we are tempted to judge, give us understanding. When opportunities arise to show Your love, help us respond faithfully.

May our words, actions, and attitudes point others toward You.

Amen.

Reflection Questions

  1. Who first demonstrated the love of Jesus to you?
  2. Where is God inviting you to show Christlike love this week?
  3. How can your daily actions reflect the love of Jesus in practical ways?

Weekly Challenge

Choose one intentional act of kindness this week.

Write an encouraging note, make a phone call, visit someone who may be lonely, or offer practical help to someone in need.

Then pause and reflect on how God used that simple act to make a difference.
